4.4.2. Hindi Films

4.4.2.1. Google's AI - Gemini

Here is an annotated list of 25 prominent Hindi films that are either set in, deeply centered around, or heavily feature the culture, history, and life of Punjab.

  1. Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge (1995)

Though it starts in London, the heart of this iconic romance beats in the lush mustard fields of Punjab. It defined the NRI yearning for home and established Punjab as the ultimate symbol of traditional Indian family values and romance.

  1. Rang De Basanti (2006)

This modern classic masterfully contrasts a contemporary group of friends with historical revolutionaries. Key portions are set in rural Punjab, utilizing locations like the Doraha Fort near Ludhiana to evoke an untamed, passionate spirit of patriotism.

  1. Jab We Met (2007)

The second half of this beloved romantic comedy introduces audiences to the energetic, loud, and fiercely loving family of Geet in Bhatinda. It captured the vibrant, warm hospitality of a Punjabi household flawlessly.

  1. Udta Punjab (2016)

Moving far away from typical Bollywood glamour, this gritty crime drama addresses the dark reality of substance abuse and political corruption affecting the youth of the state. It is an intense, unflinching look at a modern social crisis.

  1. Veer-Zaara (2004)

Yash Chopra’s grand cross-border love story splits its time between Pakistan and the vibrant villages of Indian Punjab. Through sweeping songs like "Aisa Des Hai Mera," the film acts as a gorgeous love letter to Punjabi culture, agriculture, and festivals.

  1. Bhaag Milkha Bhaag (2013)

A powerful biographical sports drama based on the life of Milkha Singh, the "Flying Sikh." The film traces his traumatic childhood during the Partition in Punjab, his journey through the Indian Army, and his rise to becoming an iconic international athlete.

  1. Amar Singh Chamkila (2024)

Directed by Imtiaz Ali, this musical biopic chronicles the meteoritic rise and tragic assassination of the highest record-selling Punjabi artist of his time. Shot on location in the rustic, real villages of Punjab, it deeply explores the socio-political climate of the 1980s.

  1. Maachis (1996)

Directed by Gulzar, this critically acclaimed political thriller is set against the backdrop of the mid-1980s Punjab insurgency. It explores the human cost of systematic failure and how ordinary Punjabi youths were driven toward militancy.

  1. Sardar Udham (2021)

A masterful historical drama tracking the life of revolutionary Udham Singh, who assassinated Michael O'Dwyer in London to avenge the horrific Jallianwala Bagh massacre of Amritsar. The film's recreation of 1919 Punjab is brutally authentic and emotionally shattering.

  1. Border (1997)

J.P. Dutta’s war epic tells the story of the Battle of Longewala. The film prominently features characters belonging to the Punjab Regiment, highlighting the immense bravery, marital history, and sacrificial military culture deeply embedded in Punjabi families.

  1. Dangal (2016)

Though based on a true story from the neighboring state of Haryana, this record-breaking sports drama was shot extensively in the real villages of Ludhiana, Punjab. The local architecture and rustic gymnasiums perfectly substituted for the rural wrestling heartland.

  1. Satluj (2026)

Initially known as Punjab '95, this hard-hitting biographical drama stars Diljit Dosanjh as the revered human rights activist Jaswant Singh Khalra. The film focuses on his dangerous quest to expose the mass illegal cremations that took place during Punjab's militancy years.

  1. Rab Ne Bana Di Jodi (2008)

Set entirely in the historic city of Amritsar, this romantic drama explores the life of a simple, everyday Punjab Power employee and his vivacious young wife. The city's famous streets and the Golden Temple serve as central narrative backdrops.

  1. Gadar: Ek Prem Katha (2001)

A massive blockbuster set during the chaotic Partition era, following a Punjabi Sikh truck driver who falls in love with a Muslim aristocrat. The film captures the raw trauma and explosive emotions felt along the newly carved borders of Punjab.

  1. Dev.D (2009)

Anurag Kashyap’s contemporary spin on Devdas transposes the protagonist’s roots to wealthy, rural Punjab. The film highlights the stark dichotomy of massive ancestral wealth, modern tractors, and the underlying vices of the state’s elite youth.

  1. Luv Shuv Tey Chicken Khurana (2012)

India's first official "food film" is a quirky, warm comedy set in a small Punjabi village. It revolves around a dysfunctional family trying to recover a secret, legendary recipe for a chicken dish that once made their local dhaba famous.

  1. Manmarziyaan (2018)

Set in the bustling lanes of Amritsar, this film presents a messy, vibrant, and highly modernized love triangle. It steers clear of old stereotypes, showcasing instead how contemporary Punjabi youth navigate modern relationships, desire, and tradition.

  1. Jogi (2022)

A tense historical drama set during the tragic 1984 anti-Sikh riots. The movie tells a harrowing story of three friends of different religious backgrounds uniting to save hundreds of lives in a neighborhood, reflecting the deep scars left on the community.

  1. Highway (2014)

Imtiaz Ali’s road movie captures a literal and emotional journey across Northern India. The early parts of the film beautifully depict the truck routes and rural landscape of Punjab, providing a liberating sense of open space.

  1. Phillauri (2017)

A unique fantasy-comedy that operates on two timelines. The historical portion is set in a golden, pre-independence rural Punjab, capturing the poetry, lifestyle, and traditional ethos of a bygone era.

  1. Fukrey Return / Fukrey Franchise (Select Segments)

While primarily based in Delhi, the films rely heavily on the distinct "Delhi-Punjabi" cultural subtext, slang, and humor. Characters like Bholi Punjaban symbolize the aggressive, sharp-witted comedic archetype born of the region's diaspora.

  1. Namastey London (2007)

This romantic comedy juxtaposes ultra-modern London life with the deeply grounded, traditional values of rural Punjab. The scenes shot in the fields establish the protagonist's unshakeable connection to his roots.

  1. Gold (2018)

A sports drama about independent India's first Olympic gold medal in hockey. The film draws heavily on Punjab's historic identity as the cradle of Indian hockey, tracking players from the region navigating post-Partition loyalties.

  1. Kesari (2019)

This war epic dramatizes the historic Battle of Saragarhi, where 21 Sikh soldiers of the British Indian Army fought against 10,000 Afghan invaders. The film is a loud celebration of the legendary courage and martial honor of the Sikh soldiers from Punjab.

  1. Oye Lucky! Lucky Oye! (2008)

Set in the Punjabi-dominated neighborhoods of West Delhi, this satirical comedy follows a charismatic thief. The movie acts as a sharp cultural study of the aspirations, material greed, and specific humor of the urban Punjabi middle class.
