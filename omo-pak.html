4.1.3. Classical Music, Misc

4.1.3.1. Brave AI
4.1.3.1.1. Q
  • Classical Music In Pakistani Films
4.1.3.1.2. A
  • Pakistani film music is historically rooted in the Hindustani classical tradition, with songs derived from traditional ragas and raginis enjoying the longest cultural longevity. During the industry's golden age, particularly in the 1950s and 1960s, legendary playback singers like Noor Jehan, Zubaida Khanum, and Iqbal Bano collaborated with composers to create classics that blended classical structures with popular appeal.
  • Key examples of this classical influence in cinema include:
  • Noor Jehan and Khurshid Anwar’s collaboration in films like Koel (1959) and Ghoonghat (1962), which fused the classical ethos of the ghazal with the facility of the geet.
  • Salim Iqbal’s compositions, such as "Rim jhim rim jhim pare phoaar" and "Chanda tori chandni mein jiya jala jaye re," which retained complex, haunting classical tunes.
  • The integration of East Pakistani (Bangladeshi) composers like Muslehuddin, who infused the industry with rich Bengali folk and classical sounds.
  • The decline of the film industry in the late 1970s and the subsequent rise of ghazal and pop music as independent art forms marked a shift away from this cinematic classical tradition, though early film scores remain a significant repository of Pakistan's musical heritage.
